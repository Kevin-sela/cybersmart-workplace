First steps: spot a suspicious message

A phishing message tries to persuade you to share information, open a file, or visit a website. It may arrive by email, text, or a workplace chat. The message can look routine, so pause and check it before acting.

LOOK FOR THE CLUES

1. An unexpected request. The message asks you to sign in, share a code, pay an invoice, or act urgently.
2. A sender that does not quite match. Check the full address or account name, not only the display name.
3. A link that leads somewhere unexpected. On a computer, hover to preview the destination. On a phone, press and hold only if your device safely shows the address without opening it. If in doubt, use a trusted bookmark or contact the organisation through a known channel.
4. An unusual attachment. Do not open an unexpected file just because the message looks familiar.
5. Pressure or secrecy. A request to skip normal checks is a reason to slow down.

WHAT TO DO

Do not reply, open the attachment, or follow the link while you are unsure. Verify the request using a contact method you already trust, and follow your workplace process for reporting suspicious messages. If you already clicked or shared information, contact your IT or security team promptly.

QUICK CHECK

You receive an unexpected message saying your account will close in ten minutes unless you sign in through its link. What is the safest first step?

Pause. Do not use the link. Go to the service through a known bookmark or contact your IT team using a trusted channel.
